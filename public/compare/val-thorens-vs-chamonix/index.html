Val Thorens vs Chamonix

Disclosure: this page contains affiliate links (Booking.com, GetYourGuide). If you book through them we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. It does not change what we recommend. Pages are drafted with AI assistance and checked against official sources; see how we work.

One is a high, snow-sure, ski-in/ski-out resort in the world's biggest linked ski area. The other is a historic mountain town beneath Mont Blanc with four separate ski areas. Here is how to choose.

Short verdict. Choose Val Thorens for reliable snow, easy ski-in/ski-out logistics and lots of linked intermediate terrain. Choose Chamonix for big-mountain scenery, steep and off-piste skiing, and a real town with life beyond skiing – and accept that its ski areas are not linked by lift.

Snow and season

Val Thorens sits at 2,300 m and the season often runs from late November into early May (2025-26: 22 Nov to 3 May for Val Thorens-Orelle). Chamonix's village is at 1,035 m, so the valley floor is rarely skiable; its best snow is high up, and the lower slopes can deteriorate later in the season. If snow certainty matters – early December, late March or April – Val Thorens has the clear edge.

Terrain

Val Thorens is a lift-linked gateway to roughly 600 km of pistes across Les 3 Vallées, much of it suited to intermediates who want to cover distance. Chamonix has comparable piste length in total (~155 km) but split across several separate areas that need a bus or short drive between them, and its reputation rests on steep and high-mountain terrain. Only the Aiguille du Midi (3,842 m) reaches higher than anything in Val Thorens, and it opens routes such as the Vallée Blanche for experienced, guided skiers.

Getting there and getting around

Chamonix is closer to Geneva (about 88 km vs 143 km). In Val Thorens most accommodation has direct piste access, so you ski from the door; in Chamonix you will usually take a bus, car or short walk to the lifts, in exchange for a proper town with restaurants and non-ski activities.

Who should choose which?

Val Thorens if you want near-guaranteed snow, ski-in/ski-out, a big linked area and an easier week with kids or mixed-ability groups.
Chamonix if you are an advanced skier or want guided off-piste, love dramatic scenery, or want a town base with plenty to do on non-ski days.
