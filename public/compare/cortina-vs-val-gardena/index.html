Cortina vs Val Gardena

Two Dolomites classics: Cortina, the glamorous Olympic town, and Val Gardena, a big linked ski area at the start of the Sellaronda.

Short verdict. Choose Val Gardena for variety, a big linked area and the Sellaronda. Choose Cortina for the elegant town, the scenery and a more relaxed, upscale atmosphere. This verdict rests on the figures below plus our own judgement, not on a single official ranking.

Skiing

Val Gardena officially counts 59 lifts and 115 slopes and is the start of the Sellaronda, the famous ski loop around the Sella massif; its pass links to the 12 areas of Dolomiti Superski. Cortina is also part of Dolomiti Superski, with its own local pass and a different feel: scenic, sunny and less of a lift-hopping destination.

Price

Cortina's own pass is 6 days for €421 in high season and €378 in shoulder season. The Dolomiti Superski pass, which covers both areas, is €453 for 6 days in high season (provisional announcement), so the Cortina-only pass is the cheaper option if you stay in the Cortina area.

Atmosphere

Cortina is an elegant, upscale town with a strong Olympic heritage; Val Gardena's villages feel more like ski-focused Alpine villages with Ladin culture. Pick Cortina if the evening scene and the town matter more than the lift count.

Who should choose which?

Val Gardena if you want a large linked area, variety for mixed abilities, and the Sellaronda.
Cortina if you want a glamorous town, big scenery and a more relaxed week, and you don't need the biggest lift network.
