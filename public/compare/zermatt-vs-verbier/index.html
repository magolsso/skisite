Zermatt vs Verbier

Two of Switzerland's most famous ski resorts, both in the Valais, both expensive, and very different in character: a car-free village under the Matterhorn versus a freeride capital with a famous après scene.

Short verdict. Choose Zermatt for high-altitude cruising, scenery, a linked cross-border area and a refined, car-free village. Choose Verbier for off-piste and freeride skiing and a livelier après-ski scene.

Terrain

Zermatt's ski area reaches almost 4,000 m, the highest in the Alps by the resort's own description, and links across the border to Cervinia in Italy. The wide, groomed, high runs suit intermediates, with the Matterhorn in view. Verbier's reputation rests on its off-piste: the 4Vallées area counts 410 km of ski runs and freeride routes, and the resort is known as a freeride destination. Experts wanting steep terrain and untracked snow will find more to do in Verbier.

Snow and season

Zermatt's altitude gives it the edge on reliability and length: the resort says skiing runs 365 days a year, and its winter pass runs from 1 November to 3 May. Verbier's season is November to the end of April, with its 4 Vallées pass valid from 12 December. For early or late-season trips, Zermatt is the safer choice.

Price

Both are expensive. On the 2026-27 official prices, Zermatt's 6-day winter pass starts from CHF 384, but that is the cheapest date; Verbier's full 4 Vallées pass is CHF 409 for 6 days. Zermatt can therefore be cheaper if you travel in November or late April, and similar in peak weeks. Check the price for your exact dates; accommodation costs more than lift passes in both places.

Après-ski and village

Verbier is the livelier of the two, with a famously active après-ski scene and a village you can drive into. Zermatt is car-free (you arrive by train) and more refined: a Matterhorn backdrop, upscale restaurants and hotels, and a calmer evening scene, although slopeside spots such as Hotel Cervo do offer lively terraces.

Who should choose which?

Zermatt if you are an intermediate or advanced piste skier, want the best views, high snow reliability, early or late-season skiing, or a car-free village.
Verbier if you are an expert or off-piste skier, want a livelier après scene, or want to arrive by car.

FAQ

Is Zermatt or Verbier better for beginners and intermediates?

Zermatt, in our view: a large, high-altitude, linked area with wide groomed runs. Verbier is better known for steep and off-piste terrain.

Which is cheaper?

Lift passes are similar. Zermatt's 6-day pass starts from CHF 384 on the cheapest dates; Verbier's 4 Vallées 6-day pass is CHF 409. Accommodation is expensive in both.
