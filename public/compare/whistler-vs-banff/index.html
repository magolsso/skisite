Whistler vs Banff

Disclosure: this page contains affiliate links (Booking.com, GetYourGuide). If you book through them we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. It does not change what we recommend. Pages are drafted with AI assistance and checked against official sources; see how we work.

Two Canadian classics: Whistler Blackcomb, a huge purpose-built resort with a ski-in village, and Banff, three resorts in a national park sharing one lift ticket.

Short verdict. Choose Whistler for the biggest single ski area, great vertical and a lively ski-in village. Choose Banff (SkiBig3) for dramatic national-park scenery, three different mountains on one ticket, and a quieter, drier-snow experience.

Terrain and lifts

Whistler Blackcomb is one connected area with two mountains, 36 lifts, 200+ trails and more than 1,500 m of vertical on each mountain. SkiBig3 is three separate resorts: Lake Louise alone has about 4,200 acres, and the whole group totals 7,948 acres with 32 chairlifts and 2 gondolas and 367 runs. Terrain is similar in size; Whistler is one connected mountain system, Banff is three destinations.

Snow

Both are snowy. Whistler quotes 10.4 m a year at the summit; SkiBig3 quotes about 30 feet (roughly 9 m) of dry Rockies powder. Banff is generally the colder and drier of the two, while Whistler's coastal climate means the lower mountain can be wetter.

Village and getting around

Whistler's village sits at the base of both mountains and is built for skiing. Banff is a town in a national park: you drive or take a shuttle to the three resorts, and Lake Louise is a separate area. From Vancouver, Whistler is roughly two hours by road; Banff is roughly 90 minutes from Calgary.

Who should choose which?

Whistler if you want one huge mountain system, big vertical and a ski-in village with lots of nightlife.
Banff if you want famous scenery, three resorts on one ticket and a town and national-park atmosphere.
