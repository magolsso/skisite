Epic Pass vs Ikon Pass

The two big multi-resort passes are built differently. Epic is Vail Resorts' pass, with unlimited access to the resorts it lists. Ikon is Alterra's wider network, with unlimited access at some resorts and a set number of days at others.

Short verdict. On launch price, Epic is cheaper ($1,089 vs $1,399 for the full pass). Ikon has more destinations (76) and a stronger refund policy. Pick by where you will actually ski, not by price alone.

Price

At launch the full adult Epic Pass was $1,089 and the full Ikon Pass $1,399, a $310 gap. The cheaper tiers are Epic Local at $809 and Ikon Base from $949. Both operators discount young skiers: Epic takes 20% off for ages 13–30, bringing the full pass to $869. Ikon's Squad Pack gives about 20% off if one person buys five Base Passes for friends aged 23–28 ($750 each).

How the passes work

Epic gives unlimited, blackout-free skiing at the resorts it lists and a limited number of days at participating resorts elsewhere, after which extra days are 50% off. Ikon varies by resort: some are unlimited, others offer a fixed number of days, which you should check against your plan.

Refunds and protection

Ikon's policy is the more generous: a pass never scanned by 15 January 2027 is fully refunded, and one scanned once gets 50% back. Epic includes Epic Coverage for qualifying personal events like injury or job loss, but it is not a use-it-or-refund policy.

Which to choose

If most of your skiing is at Vail Resorts' own mountains in Colorado, Tahoe, Whistler or Austria, Epic is usually the better value. If you want variety across Utah, Aspen, Jackson Hole, Japan or Zermatt, Ikon's coverage is broader. Check the current partner lists for the exact resorts and day limits.

Who should choose which?

Epic if you ski a lot at a few Vail Resorts mountains, want the lower headline price and unlimited days at your home resort.
Ikon if you want to range widely across destinations and value a generous refund if your plans change.
